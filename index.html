Welcome to the SWAN Erasmus+ Sport Project

Every rally builds a new neural connection.

SWAN brings therapeutic table tennis to people living with Parkinson's and Alzheimer's — and gives sports and health professionals across Europe the knowledge and resources to make an impact in their communities.

Neurodegenerative diseases (NDDs), including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, affect millions of Europeans. Physical exercise, such as table tennis, can reduce NDD risk and slow progression. The SWAN project aims to develop and evaluate a structured table tennis intervention for individuals with NDDs, promoting social inclusion, equal access to sports, and health-enhancing physical activity. It also seeks to educate stakeholders and create guidelines for integrating table tennis programs in various settings. The ultimate goal is to create an ecosystem to improve the well-being of individuals with NDDs across Europe, through physical activity in general and table tennis in particular.

Why table tennis

One sport, three benefits at once.

A rally requires the body and the brain to cooperate in order to solve the same problem in the same half-second. That is what makes table tennis worth adapting for NDD care — and what SWAN sets out to structure, deliver and evaluate.

Movement and balance

Short, repeated exchanges train footwork, reach, grip, balance and weight transfer — the everyday motor skills that neurodegenerative diseases often erode.

Cognitive functions

Reading spin, choosing a shot and adjusting mid-rally puts continuous demand on attention, working memory and reaction speed — cognition trained through play rather than through strict exercises.

Company and social belonging

Table tennis is played across a table, face to face. Sessions are social by design, which is what keeps people coming back and counters the isolation that so often follows a diagnosis.
