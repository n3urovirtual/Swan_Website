Spin for Well-being

Every rally builds a new connection.

SWAN brings therapeutic table tennis to people living with Parkinson's and Alzheimer's — and gives the coaches, carers and clubs across Europe the training to deliver it.

Neurodegenerative diseases affect millions of Europeans, and physical exercise can reduce the risk and slow progression. SWAN builds, delivers and evaluates a structured table tennis intervention — then writes the guidelines that let others run it in care homes, clubs and communities.

Why table tennis

One sport, three kinds of work at once

A rally asks the body and the brain to solve the same problem in the same half-second. That is what makes table tennis worth adapting for NDD care — and what SWAN sets out to structure, deliver and evaluate.

Movement and balance

Short, repeated exchanges train footwork, reach, grip and weight transfer — the everyday motor skills that neurodegenerative diseases erode first.

Attention and decision

Reading spin, choosing a shot and adjusting mid-rally puts continuous demand on attention, working memory and reaction — cognition trained through play rather than through exercises.

Company and belonging

Table tennis is played across a table, face to face. Sessions are social by design, which is what keeps people coming back and counters the isolation that so often follows a diagnosis.
