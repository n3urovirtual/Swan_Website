Event 2–4 October 2026

SWAN returns to the World Table Tennis for Health Festival

Read more Show less

After a first appearance in 2025, SWAN returns to the World Table Tennis for Health Festival with a full programme. This time the consortium brings evidence: the adapted curriculums have been delivered to participants in three countries, and the evaluation data on physical, cognitive and social outcomes is ready to share.

Across the three days the project will:

  • present the evaluation findings from the pilot interventions;
  • run open, adapted table tennis sessions for festival visitors;
  • introduce the trainer course to coaches and care staff;
  • meet organisations interested in running the programme themselves.

If you work with people living with a neurodegenerative disease and plan to attend, come and find the SWAN team — we would like to hear what would make the programme work in your setting.

Partner meeting 14 May 2026

Consortium meets to review the first round of pilot sessions

Read more Show less

The transnational meeting brought the consortium together to look at the same question from seven angles: what actually happens when an adapted table tennis session meets a real group of participants.

Coaches reported back on session length, group size, the pace of progression and the balance between drills and free play. Care partners raised the practical questions that decide whether a programme survives contact with a care setting — transport, staffing, room booking and how to keep a participant engaged on a difficult day.

The outcome: a revised session structure with shorter blocks, clearer progression steps, and a simplified set of measures for trainers to record. The updated materials feed into Work Package 4.

Milestone 20 February 2026

Adapted table tennis curriculums completed for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's

Read more Show less

The two curriculums start from the same sport and diverge where the conditions do. The Parkinson's curriculum leans on rhythm, initiation of movement, amplitude and balance. The Alzheimer's curriculum leans on orientation, sequencing, recall and the social structure of the session itself.

Both were built by the project's sport scientists and clinical advisors together, and both are written for a coach rather than a clinician: every session has a stated aim, a warm-up, a set of progressions and a fallback for a participant having a harder day.

The curriculums now move into piloting in Greece, Bulgaria and Spain, where they will be evaluated for their impact on physical, cognitive and social well-being.

Publication 30 September 2025

Research Insights published: three reports now available

Read more Show less

Led by the University of Ljubljana — Faculty of Sport, Deliverable 2.1 collects what is already known about table tennis as a health intervention, what practitioners across Europe are already doing, and what the people who would run these programmes say they are missing.

  • Best Practices Report (D2.1.1) — ITTF Foundation
  • Needs Assessment Report (D2.1.2) — KTG R&I
  • Literature Review (D2.1.3) — University of Ljubljana

All three reports are on the Resources page.

Milestone January 2025

SWAN begins: seven partners, seven countries, one shared aim

Read more Show less

SWAN — Spin for Well-being: Active lifestyles through therapeutic table tennis for individuals with Neurodegenerative diseases — brings together researchers, sport federations, care organisations and technology partners from across the European Union.

The kick-off set the shape of the work: research first, then a curriculum, then trainers, then evidence. The aim behind all four is a network of people and organisations able to keep running these programmes long after the project itself ends.

Read more about the plan on The Project page.
